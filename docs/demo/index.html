Understand the measure

What we measured, who measured it, and under which conditions.

SEO and GEO in plain words

SEO: be found on Google

Natural search optimisation. The aim is for Google to understand the site, trust it and show it when a buyer looks for a solution like ours. It depends on technique (speed, settings), on text (titles, descriptions) and on reputation (the other sites that talk about us).

GEO: be cited by the AIs

The same work for assistants such as ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Brave or Perplexity. More and more buyers ask them directly "which solution should I choose?". The AIs mostly cite buying guides, comparisons, clear pages and the press.

The 23 examiners

All are free, public and independent from us: anyone can repeat the measure at the same address. Each card explains what the tool does, what it found on 2 November, and links to the domain with its screenshot and the detail.

Appendix E

Glossary

SEO
Natural search optimisation: everything that helps Google find, understand and rank the site well.
GEO
The same work for AI assistants (ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Brave, Perplexity): being cited in their answers.
Indexing
A page being stored by Google so that it can appear in its results.
Page title (title)
The title shown in the browser tab and in blue in Google results. Not visible on the page itself.
Description (meta description)
The short text under the title in Google results. Without it, Google makes up the snippet.
H1, H2
The main heading of a page and its subheadings. Google uses them to understand the topic.
Glued words
Two words separated by a line break on screen but glued together for Google: "builtfor".
Alternative text (alt)
The description of an image, read by Google and by the screen readers of visually impaired people.
Sitemap
The list of pages you ask Google to read.
robots.txt
The file that tells robots what they are allowed to read.
301 / 302 redirect
The automatic forwarding of one address to another: 301 is permanent (Google transfers the reputation), 302 is temporary.
404 page
The "not found" page. A real 404 tells Google the page does not exist; redirecting to the home page misleads it.
Language tag (lang)
The indication, in the code, of the language of the page.
Canonical, hreflang
Hints for Google: which version of a page is the reference, and which are its translations.
Structured data (JSON-LD)
An identity card in machine language, hidden in the page: publisher name, address, date, software, author of an article.
llms.txt
A recent file, at the root of the site, that summarises the key facts for the AIs.
LCP
The time taken to display the main element of a page (often the large image). Google's target: under 2.5 s.
Core Web Vitals
Google's 3 measures of real visitors' experience: display speed, responsiveness, stability.
Security headers
Instructions the server sends to the browser (HSTS, CSP…) to block certain attacks.
Trackers, third-party cookies
Files and scripts from other companies (Google, LinkedIn…) that follow the visitor. The law requires their consent before loading them.
Search Console
Google's free tool that shows the clicks, the searches and the errors Google sees.
Wikidata
Wikipedia's free database, which Google and the AIs read to identify a company.
AI panel
The same 5 questions asked to the same AIs at each measurement, to track whether they cite us.
INP
Interaction to Next Paint: the time the page takes to react when you click or type. Good under 200 ms. It replaced FID in the Core Web Vitals.
CLS
Cumulative Layout Shift: how much the page "jumps" while loading. Good under 0.1.
TTFB
Time To First Byte: the time the server takes to send the first byte. Beyond 800 ms, everything else is late.
Server-side rendering (SSR)
The page arrives already written, instead of being built by JavaScript in the browser. Many AI robots do not run JavaScript: without server-side rendering, they see an empty page.
Compression (Brotli, gzip)
The server sends compressed pages: 3 to 5 times lighter, so faster.
Cache-Control
The header that tells the browser how long to keep an image or a script, so as not to download it again at every visit.
AI robots
Search crawlers decide citations: OAI-SearchBot (OpenAI), Claude-SearchBot (Anthropic), PerplexityBot. Training crawlers feed the models: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, CCBot. robots.txt can let each of them through or block it.
Open Graph
The tags that decide the title, description and image shown when a page is shared on LinkedIn or in a messaging app.
E-E-A-T
Experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness: what Google looks for to trust a piece of content (named authors, sources, legal notices, reviews).
Backlinks
Links from other sites to ours. It is the site's reputation in the eyes of Google and the AIs.
WCAG
The international web accessibility rules: contrast, alternative texts, keyboard navigation, forms readable by a screen reader.
